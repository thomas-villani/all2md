# benchmarks/docx — scripted DOCX ground truth

Ground truth for DOCX conversion, generated by scripting a live Microsoft Word.

Sixteen documents across eight families, generated by scripting a live Word, each one
verified against its own claims in the saved XML and re-checked after a Word re-save.
Scoring them opens the DOCX defect stream: it opened at **ten failing checks across
seven families** and reads **six across five** today, with every control passing
throughout.

    python -m benchmarks.docx

## Why a new lane

DOCX is the next fidelity frontier, and the defects are already named: tracked
changes vanished silently until this lane measured them, `HYPERLINK`/`REF`/`SEQ`
fields are never read, style-inherited numbering is lost on exactly the corporate
templates that use it,
and a free-standing `w:sdt` block will drop an author's name on the floor.

What has been missing is an instrument. The PDF arc worked because
`benchmarks/pmc` produced a defect stream *and* a gate before the fixes landed, so
every change was measured against something that did not come from us. Walking into
DOCX with only the `roundtrip` score would repeat the pre-instrument era — that
score is self-referential, and a parser and renderer that agree on a wrong reading
score perfectly.

## Why scripted, not collected

PMC works because JATS is *publisher-produced*: the ground truth and the PDF come
from the same source and neither was authored by us. There is no equivalent bucket
of DOCX files with independent structural truth beside them.

So the truth is generated instead. A case script drives Word over COM through
[`wordlive`](https://pypi.org/project/wordlive/); the script knows exactly what it
put in the document, so it writes an expected-facts record beside the `.docx`, and
**Word's own serializer produces the file** — the markup is what Word really writes,
not what a Python writer believes Word writes. That keeps the "truth and artifact
from different tools" property that makes PMC trustworthy.

It buys something PMC cannot: **paired minimal-difference documents**, two files
identical but for one construct. A collected corpus can never afford that, and it is
a far sharper control than a global mismatch test.

It also costs something, and the cost is stated up front: a scripted corpus is
*clean*. Real documents are not. The scripted corpus is the sharp instrument and
real documents (white papers, EDGAR filings) are the noisy counterpart; the lane is
not finished with only one of them.

## Posture

**Ungated on fidelity by design, crash-gated always.** The lane exists to open the
DOCX defect stream *before* the fixes, so each one is measured. The exit criterion
for gating goes in the workflow file rather than being left to drift — the PMC lane's
definition, mirrored: after the fixes land and two consecutive scheduled runs open no
new defect.

**The lane publishes per-family defect counts first** — a ledger, honest about being
a new instrument. A scored headline number waits until the fixes give it a story. The
oracle's output shape is the ledger, not a single scalar.

**Every family records both `expected_fixed` and `expected_today`.** A defect the
lane cannot yet fix is still worth recording exactly, and writing today's behaviour
down turns the ledger into a regression gate the moment a fix lands. Where today's
behaviour is unknown — tracked changes were the case, since nothing in the parser
looked at `w:ins` at all — it gets **measured on first contact, not assumed**. That
mattered: the recorded prediction for tracked changes was wrong, and wrong in the
optimistic direction (#480).

## The first reading (2026-08-31)

```
family         checks  failing
baseline            8        0
fields              4        2
formatting          2        1
notes               2        0
numbering           6        1
sdt                 1        1
tables              3        1
tracked            12        0
TOTAL              38        6
```

The count keeps going *up* while the failures go down, and that is the shape a closed
defect should have here: a stricter measure, satisfied. `baseline` opened at 6 checks
with 1 failing and now reads 8 with 0, because closing the equation defect meant
tightening the check that had passed it. `tracked` went from 4 checks to 12 for a
different reason — revisions have three resolutions and the corpus recorded all three,
so scoring only `accept` left `reject` and `mark` free to rot. The scoring side now
converts the tracked family three times and holds each resolution to its own record.

The `numbering` family shows the other thing a ledger buys: it held two cases that
both reported *"numPr lives on the style"*, and the second was compound. Fixing the
style descent flipped `style-inherited` and left `numfmt-decimal-zero` still failing —
but now failing for its own stated reason, with the list detected and demoted to
bullets rather than not detected at all. The corpus could not isolate the `numFmt`
whitelist until the defect stacked on top of it was gone.

| what is wrong | where |
|---|---|
| ~~Every tracked insertion is dropped — a paragraph made entirely of one vanishes~~ — **fixed**: revisions are resolved away on the element tree before any reader sees the document, under an `accept`/`reject`/`mark` policy | [#480](https://github.com/thomas-villani/all2md/issues/480) |
| ~~A display equation is dropped entirely~~ — **fixed**, along with a radical handler that had been silently emptying every `\sqrt` | [#481](https://github.com/thomas-villani/all2md/issues/481) |
| ~~Style-inherited numbering is not detected as a list at all~~ — **fixed**: the `w:basedOn` chain is followed, so numbering carried by a paragraph style is found | ROADMAP 21 |
| A `decimalZero` list is detected but demoted to bullets — the parser knows five of the 46 `w:numFmt` values Word writes | ROADMAP 21 |
| A `HYPERLINK` field's instruction leaks into the output as bare prose instead of becoming a link | ROADMAP 21 |
| A `SEQ` caption loses its number — the document prints `Figure 1:`, the output says `Figure :` | ROADMAP 21 |
| Boldness carried by a character style is lost | ROADMAP 21 |
| A block content control's content is dropped | ROADMAP 21 |
| A merged cell's text is duplicated across every column it spans | ROADMAP 21 |

The lane is **ungated on the defect count** and always will be — a gate on it would
punish finding things. What it does gate on is the lane being wrong: a crash, a corpus
that fails verification, or a *control* failing.

That last one earned its keep on the first run. Two controls failed, and neither was a
parser defect. One was a naming accident — `is_control` was inferred from a `-control`
filename suffix, which caught `sdt/block-content-control`, a defect case whose name
merely ends in the word; controls are now declared in the case spec. The other was the
case over-claiming: wordlive writes a markdown code span as an `rFonts Consolas` run,
a *font* with no character style and no semantic marker, and refusing to call every
monospace run `code` is correct behaviour rather than a defect. The control was
asserting something that should not be true, and the run said so.

A third correction came from the same run and is the reason
`tests/unit/test_docx_benchmark.py` exists in the shape it does. The `fields` check
originally asked whether the target URL appeared anywhere in the output — which
**passes on exactly the defect it exists to catch**, because the leaked field
instruction contains the URL. It reported a clean pass on a broken document until it
was rewritten to ask whether the target became a link, and until every rule was shown
failing on deliberately broken output.

## Layout

```
benchmarks/docx/
  README.md
  __init__.py  __main__.py         # python -m benchmarks.docx
  corpus.py                        # manifest load + digest verification (no COM)
  oracles.py                       # expected-facts vs AST comparison rules
  run.py                           # scoring orchestration
  generate/                        # HAND-RUN, Windows+Word; never imported by CI
    generate.py                    # runs the per-case scripts through wordlive
    cases/<family>/<case>.json     # wordlive ops — the generation "source"
  corpus/<family>/<case>.docx      # committed generated documents
  expected/<family>/<case>.json    # expected-facts records
  manifest.json                    # digest + size pin, plus generation provenance
```

COM dependencies (`pywin32`, `wordlive`) stay under `generate/` and **not** in the
`benchmarks` extra, which the Linux runner syncs — nothing outside `generate/` imports
`win32com`, so scoring runs on the Linux runner like every other lane.

It is `generate/` and not `build/` for a mundane but load-bearing reason: ruff, black
and mypy all exclude `build` unanchored, so a directory of that name anywhere in the
tree is silently outside all three quality gates. Naming it `generate/` keeps the
COM code linted and type-checked like everything else, without loosening an exclusion
that exists to skip real packaging artifacts.

## The manifest pins truth and document together

Per file: `sha256`, `size_bytes`, `case_id`, `family`, `generator` (the ops JSON that
produced it), and `expected_sha256` — the digest of the expected-facts record, so a
document cannot drift away from its own truth. Plus generation provenance:
`word_version`/`word_build`, `wordlive_version`, `generated_utc`, and
`word_user_name` (the pinned tracked-change author).

Schema-versioned, strictly validated on load, LF-normalized writes so digests
reproduce cross-platform.

## Matching is positional, not fuzzy

PMC aligns fuzzily because a PDF's reading order is a guess. Here the truth is exact
and ordered, so the nth heading is compared to the nth expected heading and there is
no attainable ceiling for text. Anything less would let a real defect hide inside
alignment slack.

One consequence worth naming: the DOCX parser sets `source_location` on nothing
(confirmed — zero occurrences), only `metadata["source_style"]`. Alignment is
therefore positional and text-based. Paragraph-index provenance on DOCX nodes is a
cheap parser improvement that would also feed Theme 8 Stage 3.

## Controls

An oracle that cannot fail is worthless, so four of them:

1. **Wrong-document control** — score case A's output against case B's truth;
   separation must be near-total.
2. **Mutation control** — scramble, reverse and truncate the parsed AST; every
   reported dimension must degrade.
3. **Paired minimal-difference documents** — two cases identical but for one
   construct; the score delta must track the known structural delta. Only a scripted
   corpus can afford this, and it is the strongest of the four.
4. **Word re-save stability** — re-open and re-save each generated file through Word
   and re-verify its facts. This guards the "exact truth" premise itself against Word
   normalizing something on save. Run at generation time, recorded in the manifest.

Red-path tests for the oracle rules live in `tests/unit/test_docx_benchmark.py` with
deliberately broken fixtures per rule.

## Positional truth, recorded from v1

`wordlive paragraphs` gives per-paragraph character offsets and `wordlive locate
--anchor-id` returns the page, line and column Word actually laid an anchor out at.
So expected-facts records carry Word-verified positional truth per block from v1 —
**informational and not scored** until the parser emits `source_location` for DOCX,
at which point this corpus becomes the DOCX provenance oracle at no extra cost.
Cheap now, expensive to retrofit.

`wordlive checkpoint`/`diff` and `read markdown` give Word's own reading of a
document: a second oracle sharing no code with either the generation script or
all2md.

## Size

**v1 is ~2–4 cases per family, roughly 25–30 documents** — small enough that every
truth record is hand-reviewed before it is blessed, large enough that each fix has
real coverage. It scales after the first defect stream, the way PMC went 12 to 66.

## Families

Sixteen cases across eight families:

| family | cases | what it pins |
|---|---|---|
| `tracked` | 2 | `w:ins`/`w:del` with a pinned author; one paragraph made entirely of an insertion. Scored under all three resolutions — `accept` against `accepted_text`, `reject` against `rejected_text`, `mark` against every revision's text plus a struck deletion |
| `numbering` | 3 | `numPr` on a style rather than a paragraph; `decimalZero` starting at 3; a plain numbered list as control |
| `fields` | 3 | raw `HYPERLINK` and `REF` field codes with a bookmark; a `SEQ` caption; a literal `w:hyperlink` as control |
| `formatting` | 2 | boldness carried by a character style vs directly on the run |
| `tables` | 2 | a `gridSpan` merge; an unmerged grid as control |
| `sdt` | 1 | a block content control wrapping a real paragraph |
| `notes` | 1 | a footnote and an endnote (control) |
| `baseline` | 2 | a display equation; headings and inline formatting (control) |

Six of the sixteen are **controls** — constructs that work today, so a run reporting
them broken is reporting a bug in the lane rather than in the parser. Several families
pair a defect case with its own minimal-difference control (`style-carried-bold` against
`direct-bold-control`, `field-codes` against `literal-hyperlink-control`), which is the
sharpest thing a scripted corpus can buy.

A finding already in hand for the ledger: OMML to LaTeX conversion **already exists**
in the parser (fractions, scripts, radicals and n-ary operators are correct;
matrices, delimiters, accents and function application degrade through the default
text-concat handler). So the gap is coverage, not existence.

But the display-equation case turned out not to reach it at all. This design predicted
that a wordlive-generated display equation would be *misclassified* as `MathInline`,
and said the truth records should expect that. **Measurement disproved it**: Word
writes a bare `m:oMath` as a direct child of `w:p`, which falls between both of the
parser's extraction paths, and the equation is dropped entirely
([#481](https://github.com/thomas-villani/all2md/issues/481)). The converter is fine
and is simply never called. If the fix emits `MathBlock` for a bare `oMath` alone in
its paragraph, this corpus can assert a real `MathBlock` after all — which the design
had written off as impossible to generate.
